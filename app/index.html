Campi Flegrei

An active volcanic caldera beneath Pozzuoli, on the western edge of Naples — and one of the most densely populated volcanic areas in Europe.

What you're seeing: bradyseism

The ground is slowly rising, pushed up by pressure from fluids and magma at depth. Between 2020 and 2024 the centre of the caldera rose about 700 mm — and the rate is still increasing.

The two views

  • Vertical — column height and colour show ground moving up (uplift) or down (subsidence).
  • East–West — colour shows horizontal spreading: the caldera pushes outward, so ground east of the centre moves east and ground west of it moves west.

About the buildings

OpenStreetMap footprints, shown for context. About 98% carry no height tag and use an assumed 6 m, so read them as footprints — not real heights.

How to use this

  • Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, right-drag to tilt and rotate.
  • Vertical and East–West show two different motions — up/down versus horizontal spreading. Each has its own colour scale.
  • Animate plays the uplift accumulating across 2020–2024. The first press takes a few seconds to load the full series.
  • Buildings only draw past zoom 14. Below that they're hidden deliberately, not missing.
  • Light / Dark swaps the basemap; relief reads better on Light.
  • Click any point for its displacement history.
